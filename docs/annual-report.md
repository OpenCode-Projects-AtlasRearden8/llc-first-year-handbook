# The annual-report ritual

Every state makes you prove the company still exists, on a periodic form with an unglamorous name. Indiana — and this is one of the genuinely pleasant surprises of launching here — makes you do it **every other year**, in a portal that works, for a fee under the cost of a nice dinner. This chapter is the ritual around that filing, plus the small constellation of yearly things that orbit it.

As everywhere in this handbook: **orientation only, never legal advice.** Fees, windows, and rules below are what I verified at launch in late 2026, and they *will* drift — every step links the official source, and the official source always wins.

## The Indiana facts, as of the late-2026 launch

| Fact | Value I verified | Confirmed where |
|---|---|---|
| The filing | **Business Entity Report (BER)** — not "annual report" | Indiana SOS, [Business Entity Reports page](https://www.in.gov/sos/business/business-entity-reports) |
| Frequency for LLCs | **Biennial — every other year** | Same page ("for-profit entities file every other year") |
| When it's due | During your **anniversary month** — the month you were created | Same page; your exact due date is searchable on the [INBiz business search](https://bsd.sos.in.gov/publicbusinesssearch) |
| Early window | You can file up to **90 days before** the due month (a rule effective at the start of 2026) | Indiana SOS filing pages |
| Fee | **$31 online, $50 by mail** — always file online | Same SOS page |
| What it asks | Principal office address, registered agent and office, the member/manager names | INBiz form |
| Reminder | The SOS mails a courtesy notice the month it's due — **and you should not depend on it**; make your own calendar entry | INBiz filings pages |
| The consequence of missing | The entity goes delinquent; **after five months overdue, the Secretary of State administratively dissolves the LLC** | SOS page, in bold letters that do their job |

Read that last row twice. Indiana's enforcement for this filing is real and mechanical: miss the report, and months later your LLC is dissolved *by administrative action* — the company stops existing until you reinstate it (a fee, a form, and the delightful side effects of having, for a while, technically been a person operating a business without a company). The cure for all of it is a fifteen-minute online form once every two years. This is the best-hassle-per-compliance trade in the state system, and we take it sitting down.

## First: learn your own two dates

Your BER due date is derived from formation, so — go find it once, right now, and write it down:

1. Open the [INBiz business search](https://bsd.sos.in.gov/publicbusinesssearch).
2. Search your LLC's exact name.
3. Note the **anniversary month** and whether the *next* report is due this year or next: the first report for an Indiana LLC comes two years after formation. (Formed October 2026 — like mine — means the first BER is due in October **2028**. The date in between, October 2027, is the *ritual* month anyway, and here's why.)

## The ritual: every anniversary month, forever

Indiana doesn't need you yearly. You need yourself yearly. The **ritual** is a twenty-minute block in your anniversary month — October, for an LLC like mine — that does four things, of which the state form is only one:

1. **The state check (exists, and is cheap)** — Look at the INBiz filing record: Is the registered agent still right? Is the principal address real? Is a BER due *this* year? If yes — or if any of the stored facts changed since last year — file/update now. Fifteen minutes, $31, receipt saved to `legal/`.
2. **The people-in-your-corner check** — insurance renewal dates (see [insurance starters](insurance.md)); professional contacts' working status; and the registered agent arrangement (below).
3. **The records sweep** — does the archive from [record habits](records.md) need restructuring? Is the EIN letter still where your future self expects it? Is the backup *actually* restorable (open the backup folder, open a file, confirm it's alive)?
4. **The calendar replant** — next year's four [money dates](money-dates.md), estimated-tax dates ([taxes chapter](taxes.md)), and this very ritual: all onto next year's calendar *during* the ritual.

That's the entire "keeping the company alive" job, seen once a year, in a form you can do from a hospital break room.

## Registered agent: the one job on auto-renew

Every Indiana LLC needs a **registered agent** — the official adult whose job is to receive legal papers for the company during business hours. The three arrangements:

- **Yourself** — free; the requirement is that you personally have an Indiana street address and are generally findable during business hours. That's the default for most first-year home-based LLCs, and it's fine.
- **A commercial agent** — a paid service (tens to low hundreds of dollars a year) that gives you a separate public address, scans your mail, and keeps your home address out of the state database. Common move once privacy or travel matters.
- **Change entrepreneur's caution**: whoever it is, the agent's *jurisdictional facts* (name and address on file) need to be true at all times — agent changes are an online filing, minutes long, and part of this ritual ("is the agent arrangement still true?").

Two practical notes from my launch:

- Whatever address is on file *is public* in the state database. That's normal and survivable; commercial agents exist precisely for people who feel otherwise. Either way, know what's public before you're surprised by it.
- Physical junk (the "look what's required of you" solicitations that flood new LLCs) arrives addressed to the LLC at the agent address. The one rule stays: **read each letter — junk gets shredded, and the two-year reminder from the SOS gets honored.**

## The national-system footnote: BOI, briefly and clearly

A national parallel filing, the federal **Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI)** report under the Corporate Transparency Act, has been a moving target. I verified at launch (October 2026): a FinCEN **final rule — effective August 14, 2026 — permanently ended the BOI reporting requirement for U.S. companies**; only certain foreign companies registered to do business in the U.S. still report ([FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) carries the current state, and the [Treasury announcement](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0603) does, too). What that means for this handbook's rituals: **no BOI filing for a domestic one-owner LLC as of launch** — but put one sentence in your ritual notes: *"If the BOI rule source changes anything new, that's a professional question."* The rule's own source (FinCEN) is the entire update-chase.

## When things go sideways (the unglamorous section)

File this page under "already armed" rather than "possible futures":

- **A notice arrived and I don't understand it.** Read it once calmly; note every date in it; bring it to the [professionals chapter](professionals.md) trigger list. SOS notices have deadlines measured in weeks, not hearts.
- **I missed the report and got the warning.** File *now*, before the five-month dissolution cliff; the portal will tell you exactly what's payable across back fees. The mistake is fixable in a single sitting if not ignored.
- **It's genuinely dissolved.** The SOS "reinstate my business" path — on [INBiz business filings](https://inbiz.in.gov/business-filings) — restores the entity for a fee. Then fix whatever caused the miss (usually: a calendar that never knew the date) and let the annual ritual do its job forever after.
- **I moved, changed agents, renamed the company.** Each is a filing with the SOS, minutes long, and each is exactly the kind of fact best caught in the annual ritual rather than stumbled over in a filing week.

## The one-page ceremony

What the ritual actually looks like, pen and paper edition (printable in [the worksheets](first-30-days.md)):

1. Calendar: open INBiz, confirm anniversary month and this year's BER due/ not due.
2. If filing: confirm facts on screen are true, pay, **save receipt PDF to `legal/`**.
3. Read every SOS/IRS/DOR letter received since last year (shred the junk; act on the rest).
4. Insurance: policies current + next renewal visible ([insurance](insurance.md)).
5. Records: archive alive, backup verified real ([records](records.md)).
6. Replant next year's calendar: 4 money dates, 4 tax dates, 1 this ritual.
7. One kind sentence about the year, written on the bottom of the checklist. (Yes, this step is part of the ritual. It's in the title of this handbook.)

Fifteen minutes on the state portion. Twenty-five with the replanting. One brunch's worth of focus, once a year, and Indiana's deadline machinery never touches your life.
