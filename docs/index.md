# The First-Year LLC Handbook

A friendly manual for surviving **and enjoying** the first year of running a single-owner LLC — written from my own launch: **Asdale Dynamics, LLC, an Indiana LLC formed in October 2026.**

If I can hand you anything from that experience, it's this: the first year is not scary paperwork. It's a short list of small habits that keep the lights on, done on a gentle schedule, mostly from your kitchen table. This handbook is that list, in plain English, step by step, never condescending. The same way [dylanasdale.github.io](https://dylanasdale.github.io) explains AI — like a friend would.

> **The deal (read this once):** This whole handbook is **orientation and education only — never legal, tax, or financial advice**. I'm a nurse-turned-AI-author, not your lawyer or your CPA. Every number in here is a plain round example to build a mental model, not a rule for your specific situation. Laws and fees change; every chapter points you to the official source to confirm. When a decision gets real, the last chapter tells you who to call.

## Who this is for

The one-person LLC that just wants a calm, boring, well-organized first year:

- The author publishing books and guides (see also the [small-business-ai-playbook](../small-business-ai-playbook))
- The tool-builder selling small apps and templates
- The side-project that quietly turned into income
- Anyone with ADHD-flavored executive function who wants rituals, not campaigns

Every chapter here is written to work at **low energy**. Where there's a hard way, there's also a gentle way, and you're welcome to it.

## The first-year arc, honestly

Here's what year one actually looks like when it goes well:

1. **Week one.** EIN, bank accounts, a folder named `ASDALE-DYNAMICS-LLC`, a bookmark of the Indiana [INBiz](https://inbiz.in.gov/) portal. Two hours, total.
2. **Weeks two through four.** Insurance quotes, a ledger, and the first [quarterly money date](money-dates.md) on the calendar. (See the [first-30-days checklist](first-30-days.md).)
3. **The quiet middle.** Ten minutes a week of records. Four money dates a year. One small estimated-tax pot you top up so you never have to guess.
4. **Your anniversary month every year.** A fifteen-minute state filing ritual (Indiana's is a real treat — it's due only every *other* year) and a check of the people in your corner: agent, insurance, records. (See [the annual-report ritual](annual-report.md).)
5. **Somewhere along the way.** One moment that genuinely warrants a professional, handled early because the [triggers chapter](professionals.md) told you it was a trigger.

That's the whole game. No heroic weekends, no April panic.

## The three habits that carry the year

Everything in this handbook serves exactly three habits:

1. **Keep the money separate.** Business dollars live in business accounts. (Chapter: [Bank accounts](bank-accounts.md).)
2. **Record as life happens.** One ledger, ten minutes a week, receipts in four folders a year. (Chapter: [Simple record habits](records.md).)
3. **Look up once per quarter.** One scheduled money date runs tax pots, subscriptions, insurance checks, and the calendar. (Chapter: [Quarterly money dates](money-dates.md).)

If you do nothing else this year, do those three. The rest is detail — pleasant detail, I promise.

This manual **complements, and deliberately does not repeat**, its two siblings:

- **[cashflow-simple-ledger](../cashflow-simple-ledger)** — the *tool*. How to record every dollar locally in a portable CSV ledger, with imports, categories, and a tax-season export. This handbook's [record habits chapter](records.md) is the *habit* around that tool.
- **[small-business-ai-playbook](../small-business-ai-playbook)** — the *co-pilot*. The monthly money-date, decision journal, SOPs, and where AI helps (drafts, summaries, reviews) and where it must never be trusted (tax numbers, legal judgment). This handbook's quarterly money date is the deeper, slower sibling of that monthly one.

Between the three: the ledger tracks the money, the playbook helps you *think*, and this handbook makes sure the *state, the IRS, the calendar, and the insurance company* stay quietly on your side.

## How to use this handbook

1. **Read it once, cover to cover, in one evening.** It's short on purpose. You'll finish feeling like the year already happened.
2. **Then live from the [checklists](first-30-days.md)** — first 30 days, quarterly money date, year end. The chapters are for the *why*; the checklists are for the *do*.
3. **When something is a link to an official source, click it and read the source.** That's the entire tax-law-update policy of this handbook: the source pages live at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov), [INBiz](https://inbiz.in.gov/), and the [Indiana DOR](https://www.in.gov/dor/), and they change.

One last note from me, person to person: forming an LLC was one of the calmest administrative experiences I've had — and I've had, uh, a *lot* of paperwork experiences. It'll be fine. Onward.
